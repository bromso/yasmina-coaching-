This statement describes how accessible the website is, what we know does not work yet and how to tell us when something is hard to use. It covers the website, the blog, sign-in, the app, the account pages and these legal pages.

## How we check accessibility

Every time the website changes, automated tests run in a real browser:

- Every page is checked with the axe tool against WCAG 2.0, 2.1 and 2.2 at levels A and AA, in both the light and the dark theme, and in Swedish and English where the page exists in both languages.
- A few paths through sign-in and the account are done with the keyboard alone: signing in, renaming a workspace and using the account menu. The tests check that you can always see where you are on the page, and that a menu keeps focus inside it and returns it when you press Escape.
- Every page is shown 320 pixels wide, as on a small phone, and must not make you scroll sideways.
- The start page and a blog post are shown with the reduced-motion setting on, and nothing on the page may keep moving.

Automated tests do not find everything. How the pages work with a screen reader, whether image descriptions are meaningful and how easy the texts are to understand need a person to judge. ## Known gaps

We know about two gaps and are working on fixing them. The third item is a test finding we have reviewed:

- In the table of contents on the legal pages, the outline that shows where the keyboard is can be cut off at the edge.
- The tables on the legal pages can be reached with the Tab key but have no name, so a screen reader does not say what the table is about when you get there.
- When the account menu is open, the rest of the page is hidden from screen readers while focus stays in the menu. The test tool flags this, but since the hidden content cannot be reached while the menu is open, we have judged that it does not stop anyone.
